# XV-15 Phase 2 data and assumptions

TMPE is an educational prototype. The wide audit, rotor CSV and aero CSV record every evaluated timestep. The checkpoint CSV gives a smaller state summary. Blank numeric fields represent unavailable quantities. Source fields identify the equations and inputs used, and status fields distinguish a load component, a partial subtotal, and a force used to advance the trajectory.

## Run modes and the 0 s stop

The [archived audit](../output/old/stage2_audit_8ur9sg5w_reports/stage2_audit_8ur9sg5w.csv) records Phase 1 ending at 118.1 m AGL and 47.25 m/s up after 5 s. The synthetic 15° collective input gives `C_T = 0.009 + 0.001(15 − 4) = 0.020`. With 565 rpm, 1.225 kg/m³ air density, vertical nacelles and the software-test load assumptions, each rotor gives 56,776.7 N. After weight, the net upward force of 55,726.5 N on 5,896.7 kg gives 9.45045 m/s². From rest this gives `v = at` and `h = at²/2`, matching the retained state. The illustrative calculation omits power limits, inflow response and rotor-on-wing download. It supplies no validated takeoff initial condition.

On the strict NASA path, the airborne fixture stops at Phase 2 t = 0. With pitch omitted, the first missing input is `--transition-pitch-deg` for the body and mast airflow. With 0° pitch entered, the first reported missing input is left mast-axis `H` for `C_RF`. Mast-axis `Y` and the right rotor quantities are also unavailable. The audit marks the request `NOT ADVANCED` and retains the initial state.

The prescribed example sets 25 m/s forward, 0 m/s up, 20 m AGL and level pitch. Both nacelles ramp from 75° to 0° over 10 s. The audit retains the original Phase 1 state and records the sources of the actual Phase 2 inputs.

```sh
python3 'MY_physics_engine/tiltrotor_modeling_physics_engine(TMPE).py' \
  --rotor-model unsupported --duration 5 --transition-duration 10 \
  --transition-kinematic 25:0 --transition-start-height-m 20 \
  --transition-start 75:75 --transition-end 0:0 --transition-pitch-deg 0
```

With the environmental defaults accepted, this case produces 101 rows at 0.1 s spacing, including t = 0 and 10 s. The final position is 250 m forward and 20 m AGL. Complete body forces and moments remain blank while independent available terms appear in the audit.

## Rotor loads and aircraft motion

The rotor audit projects each rotor's local mast loads `(−H, +Y, −T)` by transposing the body-to-mast velocity mapping. With body axes forward/right/down, `s = −1` for the left rotor and `+1` for the right, `β_m` measured from vertical toward forward, and `φ_m` the local lateral cant, the components in newtons are `F_x = T sin β_m − H cos β_m`, `F_y = s[(T cos β_m + H sin β_m) sin φ_m + Y cos φ_m]`, and `F_z = −(T cos β_m + H sin β_m) cos φ_m + Y sin φ_m`. For the modeled mast range 0–90°, each result requires only its nonzero inputs. The A-10 load definitions, A-13 Fig. A1-1 and A-18–A-19 mapping establish both the H and thrust signs. Positive thrust at vertical mast points body-up. At horizontal mast, positive H points body-up in the uncanted model. [NASA CR-166536, pp. A-10, A-13, A-18–A-19 and A-50](https://rotorcraft.arc.nasa.gov/Publications/files/CR-166536_882.pdf)

- The 1° GTRS lateral cant comes from right-shaft `PHIM`. At this cant the Y load enters body down and the axial load contributes to body right with opposite signs on the two sides. Each rotor contributes once. With a signed CG-to-hub arm `(x,y,z)` in metres the force moments are `L = y F_z − z F_y`, `M = z F_x − x F_z`, `N = x F_y − y F_x` in N·m. Positive signs are right-wing-down, nose-up and nose-right. Shaft and flapping couples are separate from the arm moments. [NASA CR-166535, p. I-2](https://rotorcraft.arc.nasa.gov/Publications/files/CR-166535_881.pdf) · [NASA CR-166536, pp. A-10, A-18–A-19 and A-33](https://rotorcraft.arc.nasa.gov/Publications/files/CR-166536_882.pdf)
- Lu et al. p. 5 Eqs. (5)–(7) are the source of the proposed `F_XR`, `F_ZR` and `M_R` expressions. The paper's Eq. (6) gives positive vertical rotor thrust at `β_m = 0°` even though Eq. (2) gives positive down gravity. That sign combination cannot be used with the NASA body-down convention and positive thrust magnitudes. Eq. (7) couples the loads to its own `l1/l2` geometry. Eqs. (8)–(9) show `½ρπR⁴Ω² C × 2` in the symmetric twin-rotor model. The scale has dimensions of force, and `×2` accounts for the pair in those equations. NASA's `DN` instead applies to individual rotor loads, which the TMPE diagnostics project once per rotor. [Lu et al., 2022, §2](https://www.mdpi.com/2226-4310/9/3/155)
- The TR-1195-1 A-31 model printout gives a check using sourced loads at 80 kt, 589 rpm and 15° mast angle. The left-rotor projection gives about +1027.82 lbf forward and −4403.35 lbf down. A-32's per-rotor values are about +1027.53 and −4403.37 lbf. The agreement checks signs and the per-rotor interpretation at that trim point. [NASA CR-166537, pp. A-31–A-33](https://rotorcraft.arc.nasa.gov/Publications/files/CR-166537_883.pdf)
- **3-DoF remains unavailable.** Pitch evolution would need initial pitch and pitch rate, the total nose-up moment about the operating CG, and a matched pitch inertia and CG model through conversion. Required contributions include rotor hub moments and matching arms, wing/pylon, fuselage, tail and elevator moments, and configuration-dependent inertia terms. The XML geometric pivot offsets do not establish the required operating hub arms. `inertia.xml` reports `I_yy` of 13,200 slug·ft² at 0° nacelle and 14,300 at 90° for its 13,000 lb configuration. A separate GTRS trim output lists `IY = 21,191.4 slug·ft²` at 75° nacelle. These are isolated conditions without a common inertia and CG model. CR-166536 describes configuration-dependent CG and inertia on pp. 42–43 and A-165–A-171. [NASA CR-166536](https://rotorcraft.arc.nasa.gov/Publications/files/CR-166536_882.pdf) · [NASA CR-166537, p. A-33](https://rotorcraft.arc.nasa.gov/Publications/files/CR-166537_883.pdf)

CR-166536 pp. 11 and 43 define yaw, pitch, roll Euler rotation order and identify the aircraft motion subsystems. TMPE evaluates the equivalent 3-2-1 rotation in SI, with `R` mapping local heading-level forward/right/down to body. The translational expression is `dv_body/dt = F_body/m + R(0,0,g) − ω × v_body` with `v_body` the ground-relative velocity and `F_body` the complete non-gravity body force. The audit records standard gravity `g = 9.80665 m/s²`, the gravity projection and the independent rate coupling `(rV−qW, pW−rU, qU−pV)`. It keeps the derivative blank if the complete force for that axis is unavailable. The Euler diagnostic maps body rates to `θ̇ = q cos φ − r sin φ`, `ψ̇ = (q sin φ + r cos φ)/cos θ` and `φ̇ = p + ψ̇ sin θ`; the roll and yaw mappings remain unavailable at Euler singularities. The position calculation rotates the heading-level displacement into north/east and records altitude AGL. The motions are local and use constant gravity. The report identifies the motion methods in Appendix A subsystems 10c–f and 11–14 and references Etkin for the rigid-body equations. [NASA CR-166536, pp. 11, 43, 51 and Appendix A pp. A-2–A-3](https://rotorcraft.arc.nasa.gov/Publications/files/CR-166536_882.pdf)

| Forward and vertical | Both installed rotor force components, matched RPM/collective/inflow, complete wing, wake, fuselage, nacelle, and tail forces. The partial load subtotal names exactly which components contributed, and the demonstration advances these two axes only. |

## Phase 1 and CR-177436 evidence

- The bundled collective model remains uncalibrated. Its explicit synthetic fixture uses `C_T = 0.009 + 0.001 × (theta_deg − 4°)` and a provisional −10° to 20° software input interval. The per-rotor equation `T = C_T rho pi R² (Omega R)²` matches the convention in CR-177436. At the illustrated 15°, `C_T=0.020` is about 21% above the suggested hover maximum 0.0165, and exceeds the reported 0.0161 onset of rapidly rising alternating loads. The terminal and audit record this coefficient comparison. The installed takeoff law remains uncalibrated and applies no limiting cap. `prototype-fixed-ct` is an existing lower-thrust mechanics exercise with XML prototype `C_T=0.0102`, independent of collective.
- CR-177436 §7.1 and Fig. 7.1 describe a wind-corrected power coefficient, six runs in the discussion and a faired mean. Fig. 7.2 and the text show peak figure of merit 0.791 near `C_T=0.0105` for the original full-scale XV-15 metal-blade rotor. Fig. 7.3 uses blade angle at 0.75R. Its Ames runs show 582.8–586.2 rpm and tip Mach 0.688–0.691. The authors use the suspect Ames collective readings for curve shape, and report support for the separate WPAFB data from calculations and flight experience. The two sets differ by about 4°. The WPAFB collective has no tower-blockage correction. A verified pilot-command-to-blade-angle mapping and matched installed climb conditions are missing. No curve was digitized or extrapolated.
- CR-177436 §7.1 pp. 50, 53 concerns isolated metal-blade hover with solidity 0.089. Its suggested maximum `C_T ≈ 0.0165` is specific to that configuration. The baseline ATB has solidity 0.10 and reached about `C_T = 0.022` at reduced tip speed in §7.2 p. 62. Those measurements describe different blade and test conditions. Neither data set supplies installed transition thrust or power, and no coefficient cap was added. [NASA CR-177436, §§7.1–7.2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19880016983/downloads/19880016983.pdf)
- The reported `C_P = C_P0 + k C_T^(3/2)/sqrt(2)` uses `k=1` for ideal induced efficiency and a zero-thrust extrapolation to determine `C_P0` from matching test data. Neither coefficient is inferred for this run. CR-177436 §7.1, Figs. 7.6 and 7.7, reports no clear trend across tip Mach 0.60–0.73, with a tendency toward reduced performance at higher Mach. This observation supplies no numerical correction here.
- The §7.1 wake-rake notes place the rotor-to-wing distance at 0.40R at 75% radius. At `C_T=0.0116`, Fig. 7.9 shows contraction to about 0.79R at 0.55R downstream. At the 0.4R probe the vortex is near 0.80R. Fig. 7.10 shows an edge near 80% radius for the plotted cases. These are configuration-specific observations, separate from the local velocity and loads required to model an installed wing.

## How to improve these assumptions

- Verify the H-bar coefficient and signs from legible CR-166536 (TR-1195-2 Rev. A) pp. A-30–A-31. Recover blade twist integrals and matched root collective. Cross-check both rotor frames against Fig. A1-1 and another published trim condition.
- Obtain operating cyclic, flapping, rotor RPM, ground clearance, wake factors and hub loads for a matched XV-15 configuration. Verify blade inertia and the coning spring independently of the flapping spring. Use matched CG-to-hub offsets where rates are nonzero.
- Obtain raw metal-blade thrust/power/pitch pairs with clear pitch datum, tower and wind corrections, temperature, density and RPM. Establish installed climb/transition applicability before calibrating thrust or fitting `C_P0` and `k`.
- Recover configuration-matched wing/other airframe data and local wake fields, with the reference area, AoA and Mach ranges checked. Compare against more than one trim and independent dynamic flight histories before calibrating moments, pitch response or aerodynamic damping.
